Glowcast Back to the forecast

How it works

Glowcast reads weather forecasts and estimates how colorful each sunrise and sunset will be. Here is what goes into it, and what is measured versus estimated.

What makes a colorful sky

At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels a long way through the air, and most of the blue light scatters out along the way. What reaches the clouds is orange and red. Glowcast looks at three things that affect how much of that color you see:

  • High clouds above you. They catch the colored light. Sunset photographers and forecasters often say partial cover, around 30 to 70%, works best. A clear sky gives a glow along the horizon but little color above.
  • Nothing blocking the sun. Thick low clouds near the horizon, toward the sun, can stop the light before it reaches the clouds above you. Glowcast checks the forecast at 60, 150 and 300 km in the sun's direction.
  • Clear air. Humid or hazy air softens the colors. A little dust or smoke can deepen reds, and a lot dims everything. Rain usually means a gray sky.

Where the data comes from

All weather numbers come from Open-Meteo, which provides forecasts from national weather services. Glowcast uses four of them and compares their results:

  • ECMWF, the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts
  • GFS, from the US National Weather Service
  • ICON, from Germany's weather service (DWD)
  • GEM, from Environment Canada, which uses its 2.5 km high-resolution model (HRDPS) over North America

Smoke and dust levels come from the Copernicus atmosphere forecast, also through Open-Meteo, and only reach about 5 days ahead.

What is measured and what is estimated

Sunrise and sunset times, and the sun's direction
Calculated with the NOAA solar formulas. These are reliable to within about a minute.
Clouds, humidity, visibility, rain, smoke and dust
Taken directly from the weather forecasts. They are forecasts, so they can be wrong, especially more than 2 days ahead.
The color score (0 to 100)
An estimate. The factors above are known causes of colorful skies, but how much each one counts is Glowcast's own choice. No public, tested formula exists, and the score has not yet been checked against real sunsets.
Confidence
A simple rule, not a measurement. It is high within about a day, medium up to about 3 days and low after that. It drops a step when the four forecasts are more than 15 points apart, and goes to low when they are more than 30 apart.
The sky picture
Drawn with a physical model of sunlight passing through the air: scattering by air molecules and by haze, absorption by ozone, and the Earth's shadow. The haze level comes from the smoke and dust forecast. Clouds sit at their real heights (about 1.5, 4 and 8 km) in the forecast amounts, and each is lit by sunlight as it reaches that height. The cloud shapes are random, so it is still an illustration, not a photo.
Where to watch
Only places whose view direction is stated by a linked source, such as Tourisme Montréal or the City of Montréal.

Words on the page

Golden hour
When the sun is between 6° above the horizon and sunset (or from sunrise until it reaches 6°).
Blue hour
When the sun is 4° to 6° below the horizon.
Score labels
Poor (under 28), Fair (28 to 44), Good (45 to 61), Vivid (62 to 79), Spectacular (80 and up).
