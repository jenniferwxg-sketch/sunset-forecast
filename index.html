      How the forecast works

      Sunset and sunrise color comes from sunlight hitting clouds from below after a long, low trip through the air. Glowcast checks three things from the hourly forecast at the exact minute of sunrise or sunset:

      1. A canvas. High and mid clouds overhead give the light something to color. Around 30 to 70% cover works best. A clear sky gives a clean gradient but little drama.
      2. An open light path. Low cloud 60 to 300 km toward the sun can block the light before it arrives. Glowcast checks cloud forecasts at those points in the sun's direction.
      3. Clean air. Humid, hazy air washes colors out. A little smoke or dust deepens reds, and a lot dims everything.

      Like a real forecaster, Glowcast checks more than one weather model: ECMWF (Europe), GFS (United States), ICON (Germany) and GEM (Canada, which uses the 2.5 km HRDPS model across North America). Each one predicts cloud at three heights for every hour. Glowcast checks those clouds above you and at 60, 150 and 300 km toward the sun.

      The preview image is drawn from those numbers, so treat it as a likely look rather than a photo. Cloud forecasts more than 2 days out change often.
