    Why this score

      Weather models

      Glowcast scores the sky with each model and shows the middle score. Confidence is a rule, not a measurement: High within about 30 hours, Medium up to about 3 days, Low after that, and one step lower when the models are more than 15 points apart (Low when more than 30).

        How the forecast works

        Sunset and sunrise color comes from sunlight hitting clouds from below after a long, low trip through the air. Glowcast checks three things from the hourly forecast at the exact minute of sunrise or sunset:

        1. A canvas. High and mid clouds overhead give the light something to color. Sunset photographers and forecasters often cite partial cover, around 30 to 70%, as best. A clear sky gives a clean gradient but little color on clouds.
        2. An open light path. Low cloud 60 to 300 km toward the sun can block the light before it arrives. Glowcast checks cloud forecasts at those points in the sun's direction.
        3. Clean air. Humid, hazy air washes colors out. A little smoke or dust deepens reds, and a lot dims everything.

        Glowcast checks four weather models: ECMWF (Europe), GFS (United States), ICON (Germany) and GEM (Canada, which uses the 2.5 km HRDPS model across North America). Each one predicts cloud at three heights for every hour. Glowcast checks those clouds above you and at 60, 150 and 300 km toward the sun.

        These three factors are well known causes of colorful skies. How much each one counts in the 0 to 100 score is Glowcast's own choice. No public, tested formula exists, and the score has not yet been checked against real sunsets.
